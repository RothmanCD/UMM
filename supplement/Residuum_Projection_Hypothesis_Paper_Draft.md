## Abstract

We investigate whether residual geometric charges and a density-dependent amplitude arise as the natural mathematical projection of an effectively infinite relational structure onto a finite number of consensus dimensions. Beginning from a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space (or a unital C*-algebra), an ergodic group of *-automorphisms, and a pure invariant state, we isolate the minimal additional structures that force three phenomena: a residual discrete spectrum labeled by irreducible representations of a compact group; a saturating amplitude controlled by a local packing parameter; and a directed partial order (the arrow of differentiation) generated by the instability of the pure state.  

The residual spectrum is forced by the mere existence of one finite-dimensional invariant subalgebra. The algebraic structure of spin requires one of three independent minimality principles. The packing amplitude is obtained from the unique normalized trace on the consensus algebra; continuous fields of such algebras convert the packing parameter into a local geometric quantity. The arrow is realized by a modular-compatible class of faithful weights normalized by that same trace; its outer dynamics is completely canonical via the continuous core and the flow of weights.  

The three constructions interlock through a single residual geometry. The external parameters that remain are minimal and purely algebraic. The resulting calculus is domain-general and may be applied, without ontological commitment, to high-dimensional continuous spaces that arise in contemporary embedding models of mathematical knowledge.

## 1. Introduction and success criteria

The Residuum Projection Hypothesis asks whether residual geometric charges and a density-dependent amplitude can be recovered as the inevitable mathematical projection of an effectively infinite relational structure onto a finite number of consensus dimensions, and what mathematical object makes that projection forced rather than optional.

Three success criteria are imposed from first principles:

1. A residual spectrum that yields discrete labels carrying the algebraic structure of spin quantum numbers.  
2. A natural amplitude that depends on a local packing or density parameter and saturates at high density.  
3. A clear mathematical reason why a pure “nothingness” (0-state) is unstable once any non-zero structure is admitted, thereby generating a directed partial order—the arrow of differentiation.

The programme begins from data that contain no preferred finite-dimensional subalgebra and no residual compact group. All additional structure is introduced only when it is shown to be necessary, and is kept as weak as possible.

## 2. Primitive data

We begin exclusively with:

- a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\) (or a unital C*-algebra \(\mathcal{A}\) acting non-degenerately on it);  
- a group \(G\) of *-automorphisms of \(\mathcal{A}\) whose action is ergodic on the pure-state space;  
- a pure \(G\)-invariant state \(\Omega_0\).

Write \(M=\pi_{\Omega_0}(\mathcal{A})''\). Because \(\Omega_0\) is pure the GNS representation is irreducible, so \(M\simeq B(\mathcal{H})\) is a type I\(_\infty\) factor. No preferred finite-dimensional subalgebra and no residual compact group are assumed a priori.

## 3. Residual discrete spectrum

**Theorem 3.1.**  
Suppose there exists at least one non-trivial finite-dimensional \(G\)-invariant C*-subalgebra \(\mathcal{A}_0\subset\mathcal{A}\). Then:

1. the continuous image of \(G\) in \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A}_0)\) is a compact Lie group \(K\);  
2. the unique \(K\)-average yields a conditional expectation \(E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_0\);  
3. the residual map \(\Pi=\mathrm{id}-E\) produces residual operators whose spectral decomposition (by the Peter–Weyl theorem) is discrete and labeled by the irreducible representations of \(K\).

Thus a residual spectrum carrying the representation theory of a compact group is forced once a single non-trivial finite-dimensional invariant subalgebra is admitted. No further assumptions are required for discreteness or for compact-group labeling.

Let \(N=\mathcal{A}_0''\) and write \(\tau_0\) for the unique normalized trace on \(N\). The residual geometry determined by \(\mathcal{A}_0\) will serve as the common substrate for the remaining two success criteria.

## 4. Selection of the algebraic structure of spin

The residual group \(K\) is an arbitrary compact Lie group. Isolation of \(K\simeq\mathrm{SU}(2)\) or \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\) (or of a three-dimensional representation) is not forced by the residual-projection construction. The following three principles are the weakest purely mathematical selection rules that achieve the isolation. None presupposes orientation, spin structures, or three-manifolds.

**Principle A (minimal non-abelian rank).**  
Among compact connected simple Lie groups there is a unique isomorphism class of rank 1 that is simply connected: \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\). Its adjoint form is \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\). Rank 1 is the smallest possible non-abelian rank.

**Principle B (smallest non-commutative matrix algebra).**  
The smallest non-abelian finite-dimensional C*-algebra is \(M_2(\mathbb{C})\). Its automorphism group is \(\mathrm{PU}(2)\simeq\mathrm{SO}(3)\). If the invariant subalgebra that forces \(K\) is this minimal one, then \(K\) embeds into \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\).

**Principle C (unique non-commutative associative real division algebra).**  
By the Frobenius theorem the unique such algebra is the quaternions \(\mathbb{H}\). Its automorphism group is \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\), acting irreducibly on the three-dimensional space of pure imaginaries.

Any one of these principles forces the residual spectrum to carry the algebraic structure of spin. The two-dimensional fundamental representation of \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) remains available; the three-dimensional representation appears as the adjoint or as the pure-imaginary part of the quaternions.

Examination of the residual map, the modular theory developed below, and the continuous core of \(M\) shows that none of these structures imposes a further restriction on \(K\). The residual spectrum is compatible with an arbitrary compact group; the three minimality principles remain independent and minimal.

## 5. Saturating density-dependent amplitude

After projection onto a fixed finite-dimensional consensus subalgebra the residual algebra has fixed dimension. No continuous packing parameter arises from residual operators or from a single modular spectrum alone.

**Construction.**  
For any normal state \(\omega\) the restriction \(\omega|_N\) is absolutely continuous with respect to the unique normalized trace \(\tau_0\). There exists a unique density operator \(h\in N\) such that
\[
\omega(a)=\tau_0(ah),\qquad\tau_0(h)=1.
\]
The packing parameter
\[
\rho(\omega)=\|h\|_\infty\in\Bigl[\tfrac1n,1\Bigr]
\]
(\(n=\dim N\)) is continuous in the weak*-topology. Any continuous strictly monotone function of \(\rho\) that maps onto \([0,1]\) yields a saturating amplitude; the simplest are
\[
A=1-\rho\qquad\text{or}\qquad A=\frac{\rho-1/n}{1-1/n}.
\]
Both saturate at the extremes of purity versus maximal mixing of the consensus state. The construction uses only the conditional expectation, the unique trace, and the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the restricted state.

**Locality via continuous fields.**  
A continuous field of consensus subalgebras over a locally compact Hausdorff space \(X\) consists of a continuous field of finite-dimensional C*-algebras \(\{N_x\}_{x\in X}\) together with continuous fields of conditional expectations \(E_x\) and normalized traces \(\tau_x\). Every normal state \(\omega\) then determines a continuous family of density operators \(h_x\) and a continuous local packing parameter
\[
\rho_x(\omega)=\|h_x\|_\infty.
\]
The associated local amplitudes \(A_x(\omega)\) are continuous functions on \(X\) and continue to saturate at the local extremes of purity and mixing. The continuous-field strengthening is the weakest additional structure that converts the packing parameter from a global numerical invariant into a local geometric quantity.

Fiberwise application of the residual-spectrum theorem and of the spin-selection principles (when imposed) makes the residual spectrum and the spin structure local with respect to the same continuous field.

## 6. The arrow of differentiation

Pure states are never faithful. Tomita–Takesaki theory therefore does not assign a modular automorphism group to \(\Omega_0\). Continuous dynamics or a Lyapunov function must be supplied externally. Counter-examples demonstrate that pure invariant states can be stable. Instability of the pure 0-state and a directed partial order are consequently not forced by the primitive data alone.

**Modular-compatible class.**  
A faithful normal semi-finite weight \(\psi\) on \(M\) is modular-compatible with \(N\) if \(\sigma_t^\psi(N)\subset N\) for all \(t\in\mathbb{R}\). By Takesaki’s theorem this is equivalent to \(E\) being \(\psi\)-preserving. The modular-compatible class \(\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mod}}(N,\tau_0)\) consists of all such weights that further satisfy the normalization
\[
\psi\big|_N=\tau_0.
\]
The class is non-empty. Explicit density-operator constructions (densities that normalize \(N\)) show that it contains members for which the continuous orbit
\[
t\mapsto\Omega_0\circ\sigma_t^\psi
\]
is non-constant. For every member of the class the residual map intertwines the modular group, so relative modular operators (or relative entropy) between \(\Omega_0\) and points of its orbit respect the consensus/residual decomposition and supply a Lyapunov function that orients the orbit.

The residual part of the density that determines a modular-compatible weight remains free. Four candidate selection principles that attempt to fix this residual density by means of the residual spectrum were examined; none is forced. The residual spectrum constrains the possible densities but does not eliminate the residual freedom.

**Continuous-core realization of the outer arrow.**  
The continuous core \(c(M)\) of \(M\) and the flow of weights (the dual action on the center of the core) are independent of the choice of weight. Every modular-compatible weight lifts to the core, and the residual geometry embeds equivariantly under the dual action. The modular orbit of \(\Omega_0\), once lifted, becomes an orbit under this completely canonical action. Relative modular data descend to the core. Consequently the directed partial order—the arrow of differentiation—is an invariant of the flow of weights. Different residual densities merely supply different cocycle representatives of the same outer flow. The residual density remains an internal parameter of each concrete realization, while the outer arrow is completely canonical.

## 7. Interlocking of the three criteria

All three success criteria are realized through the residual geometry determined by a (continuous field of) consensus subalgebra(s) \(\mathcal{A}_0\):

- the residual spectrum is the Peter–Weyl decomposition of residual operators;  
- the packing amplitude is constructed from the unique normalized trace on the consensus algebra;  
- the arrow is generated by modular-compatible weights normalized by that same trace, with outer dynamics given by the flow of weights.

When a continuous field of consensus subalgebras is present, residual spectrum, packing amplitude, and arrow become simultaneously local. When one of the three spin-selection principles is imposed, the residual labels acquire the algebraic structure of spin, again fiberwise if a continuous field is used. The external parameters that remain—choice of a spin-selection principle, choice of a continuous field (if locality is desired), and residual density inside the modular-compatible class—are minimal and purely algebraic.

## 10. Concluding remarks on the broader programme

The formal apparatus developed above treats the structures of quantum mechanics as a domain-general geometric calculus for high-dimensional continuous spaces. Its effectiveness does not depend on any claim about the ultimate ontology of the physical universe. Modern embedding spaces and latent spaces of large language models supply a practical computational arena in which residual projection, residual spectrum, packing-dependent amplitude, and modular arrows can be realized at scale and subjected to external verification. The Residuum Geometry Project therefore pursues two tightly coupled goals: completion of the pure-mathematical formalization, and demonstration that the resulting calculus is operationally useful for organizing high-dimensional representations of mathematical and physical knowledge.
